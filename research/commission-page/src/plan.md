# Commission & Pay Plan

Draft for Post, Brenda and Diana to review, October 2026. Every number here is a starting point to argue about, not a decision. Change a number and the structure still works.

## The short version

- **Callers earn two things on every sale:** a bonus when the client's first payment clears, and 10% of every monthly payment after that.
- **The bonus is one month of the client's plan.** On month-to-month it also includes 20% of the $299 setup fee.
- **Extras earn too:** monthly extras like the plan, one-time and per-item extras 20% of their price, priced-per-job work 20% of what we charge.
- **The app has a calculator** (Home → 💰 What I'd earn) that shows the exact amount for any plan, way to pay and mix of extras, split or not.
- **Brenda and Diana are paid from a team pool:** 15% of everything clients pay, split by the hours each of them logs. They also earn normal commission on anything they sell themselves.
- **Post owns the company.** He is paid from what is left after Stripe, commission, the team pool and running costs. Two versions follow: one where he also earns commission on his own sales, and one where he doesn't.
- **When one person opens a lead and another closes it,** the bonus splits 40% to the opener and 60% to the closer.
- **If a client cancels or gets a refund within 60 days,** the bonus comes back out of the seller's next payout.

## Where every dollar goes

Each payment from a client is divided in this order:

1. **Stripe's fee:** 2.9% plus 30¢ per charge.
2. **The seller's commission:** the bonus on the first payment, 10% on every payment after it.
3. **The team pool:** 15% of what the client paid, for Brenda and Diana.
4. **Running costs:** about $50 to $100 a month right now for Cloudflare, Google Workspace seats, Claude and the domain. These come out of the month's total, not out of each client.
5. **What's left is profit,** divided three ways: 25% set aside for taxes, 15% reinvested (ads, printing, tools, a cushion), and 60% paid to Post.

## Extras

Extras are added on top of the plan's commission. How each kind earns:

- **Monthly extras** (Social media posts, Ad management, Barcode scanning) work exactly like the plan: one month of the extra up front, then 10% of every monthly charge. On a yearly plan they're charged for 12 months at once, so the seller gets one month of the extra plus 10% of the year.
- **One-time extras** (Photo shoot, Logo refresh, Google Business Profile setup and the rest) pay 20% of their price, up front.
- **Per-item extras** (QR tags, table tents, NFC cards, Extra changes) pay 20% of the price for each one sold.
- **Priced per job** (Business cards, yard signs & door hangers) pays 20% of whatever we end up charging, when the client pays it.
- **A founding customer's free extra** pays nothing, because the client pays nothing for it.

## Model A: Post earns commission on his own sales

Post is treated exactly like any caller when he sells. He gets the bonus and the 10% residual personally, then his owner share of whatever profit is left.

**What it means on the $49 month-to-month client, year one, when Post sells it himself:**

| Who | Gets |
| --- | --- |
| Post as the seller | $162.70 |
| Post as the owner | $337.16 |
| Post in total | $499.86 |
| Team pool | $133.05 |
| Taxes set aside | $140.48 |
| Reinvested | $84.29 |

**Why choose it:**

- Every sale costs the company the same, whoever makes it, so the books show what each sale really earns.
- Post can see his own sales next to everyone else's on the same terms.
- If the company ever takes on a partner or investor, the owner's selling pay is already separated from the owner's profit.

**What to watch:**

- It is money moving from one of Post's pockets to another. For a one-owner LLC, the IRS treats all of it as Post's income either way, so tax still has to be set aside on the commission part.
- Less of each of Post's sales lands in the reinvest and tax buckets.

## Model B: Post takes no commission

When Post sells, no commission is paid. The money a caller would have earned stays in the company and becomes profit.

**The same client, year one, when Post sells it himself:**

| Who | Gets |
| --- | --- |
| Post as the owner | $434.78 |
| Team pool | $133.05 |
| Taxes set aside | $181.16 |
| Reinvested | $108.69 |

**Why choose it:**

- Simplest to run: there is one way Post gets paid.
- Post never competes with callers for commission, which keeps the split-sale rules easy and fair.
- More of each owner sale goes to taxes and reinvestment, which helps while the company is small.

**What to watch:**

- Post's personal take on his own sales is lower in the moment: $434.78 against $499.86 in the example above. The difference isn't lost; it sits in the tax and reinvest buckets.

**Recommendation: Model B.** With one owner, commission to himself mostly moves money around. Model B is simpler, keeps more cash in the company during its first year, and makes it clear to the team that Post isn't competing with them for leads.

## When a sale is shared

**Who counts as the opener:** whoever first logged real contact with the business owner in the app, meaning Reached, Interested, Shown, Talked in person, or Left a flyer.

**Who counts as the closer:** whoever sent the sign-up that got signed, or took the payment in person.

**The rules:**

- One person did both: they get the whole bonus and the residual.
- Different people: the bonus splits **40% to the opener, 60% to the closer.** The monthly residual goes to the closer, because they now own the relationship with that client.
- A lead belongs to its opener for 30 days after the opener's last logged contact. After 30 days with nothing logged, the lead is open again, and whoever closes it gets the whole commission.
- Nobody gets credit for a lead they didn't log in the app. If it isn't in the call log, it didn't happen.

## Brenda and Diana

They do the work that keeps clients paying: billing, building and fixing sites, change requests, the books. Commission alone wouldn't pay them for that, so they get the team pool.

- **The team pool is 15% of everything clients pay,** split between them by the hours each logs that month.
- **When either of them sells,** they earn the same commission as any caller, on top of their pool share.
- **Early on, the pool will be small.** If that's too little to live on, the alternative is a fixed hourly rate paid from the company until the pool is bigger than the hourly pay would be, then switching over.

At 20 clients averaging $89 a month, the pool is about $267 a month. At 100 clients, about $1,335.

## Protecting the company

- **Clawback:** if a client cancels or gets a full refund within 60 days of their first payment, the bonus comes back out of the seller's next payout. On a partial refund, the bonus is reduced by the same share.
- **Residuals stop** when the client stops paying, and start again if they come back within 90 days.
- **Payouts go out monthly,** for the payments that cleared in Stripe the month before.
- **Cap the residual at 24 months per client** so the cost stays predictable as clients pile up. Remove the cap later if you want to.

## Decisions to make

- Model A or Model B for Post.
- The bonus size: one month of the plan, or a flat amount per sale.
- The residual: 10% or a different rate, and whether it stops at 24 months.
- The split: 40/60, or 50/50.
- The team pool: 15% of revenue split by hours, or hourly pay to start.
- Whether the 60-day clawback is long enough.
