# App Review, October 2026

As of 13 October 2026 · Underground Associates, internal

Five days into real use, the app does everything from finding a business to publishing its website, and the team has touched 18 of 127 leads, shown 11 and sold none. The limit right now is daily execution, not a missing feature. This review has the week's numbers, what they mean, what comparable companies and the research say, and the changes ranked for the team to weigh in on. ## What the numbers say

The bottleneck is calls and visits per day, not software. Everything below follows from that.

**Capacity.** Four people logged 75 entries in five days, 20 of them "added by hand" notes. At five real touches a day per person the 109 untouched leads clear in about a week. At the actual pace most will expire unseen: a New lead expires 30 days after it was built, and the oldest untouched ones are already five days old.

**Follow-ups get booked and then missed.** The cadence books day 2, 5, 10 and 21 steps, and eight were overdue this morning. "Up next" shows them, but only when someone opens the app. Nothing reaches a pocket at 8 AM saying "3 callbacks and 2 visits today".

**Nobody owns a lead.** Two callers and the owner work one list; the queue shows everyone the same five best leads. There is no claim, no "this week's twenty for Fox", and no view of who dropped what.

**The texted preview is not landing.** One open from eight texts. The text goes to the number on the listing, which for a restaurant is the counter phone, not the owner's. The only real interest in the notes came from walk-ins ("very interested, going to show his wife").

**Hand-added beats run-found.** Leads someone typed in after a visit or a tip were shown at twice the rate. Warm beats cold, which argues for letting the field drive the list.

**Churches were the wrong first category.** Lowest scores, four required to-dos before a site can go live, committee decisions, a discounted plan, and none shown after a week. They belong in a slow lane once there are paying clients.

**Restaurants are the right first category, with a trap.** Every restaurant preview needs its menu typed in before it can go live, and salons need their services and prices. The first signing turns into an evening of typing for the office. The app can remove that chore (see the changes below).

**Fields exist but are not fed.** The lead has a place for who to ask for and when they are in; one lead has it. The log card does not ask after a visit, so the next person walks in blind.

**The inbox is spam.** Three of four company-site messages were SEO sellers, each one an alert on the owner's phone. The form has no spam check.

**Every feature ships twice.** The web app and the Android app both carry the full lead page, so each change costs about 1.6 times what one client would. Native was the right call for Tap to Pay and the owner's phone; it is worth deciding on purpose which screens need it.

### Keeping clients

- At $49 to 149 a month the natural churn is 3 to 5% a month unless the company behaves like a higher-priced vendor (ChartMogul). A second paid product cuts a customer's churn by as much as half (Thryv); clients not sold a second thing by month 3 churned 62% (Vendasta).
- About a third of subscription churn is failed cards (Recurly); Stripe's card updater and retries fix most of it for free.
- Annual prepay retains about 30% better and is best asked for 2 to 9 months in, not at signing. "Two months free" beats a percentage.
- At the $89 plan, 5% monthly churn means about 20 months and $1,780 per client; 3% means $2,970; GoDaddy-like retention means about $6,600.

### The rules, in plain words (worth one lawyer read)

- Alabama's Telemarketing Act licenses selling by phone: $500 a year plus $50 per caller, and selling without one is a felony. The exemption that fits us is "the call books a face-to-face meeting and the sale happens there". Closing on the phone and texting a checkout link sits outside it.
- Business-to-business calls are exempt from the federal Do Not Call registry, but a cell phone an owner also uses at home can count as residential. Keep one do-not-contact list for calls and texts and honor it within ten business days.
- Texts sent one at a time by a person from their own phone, after the owner gave the number, are the compliant pattern, which is what the app does. Put "Reply STOP" in the templates and never text a number that has not been reached live.

## The changes, in order

The first six get the leads we already have worked; nothing after them matters until they happen. Effort: S is an afternoon, M a few days, L a week or more.

| # | Change | Why | Effort |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | A 7:45 AM push to each person with the day's callbacks, visits and fresh calls, and a 6 PM count to the owner | 8 of 13 callbacks were overdue; reps left to themselves under-touch | S |
| 2 | Assign each caller 15 to 20 leads a week; claims lapse at midnight; the owner sees what nobody took | A shared queue showing everyone the same five leads invites cherry-picking; 109 leads untouched | S to M |
| 3 | Treat "opened their preview", a text back or a form post as a five-minute emergency with a push | Answering within the hour multiplies qualification about 7 times; a day later, 60 times less | S |
| 4 | Front-load the follow-up: text within the hour of a demo, call next day, then the existing steps; show "attempt 3 of 6" on the lead | The optimum is 6 attempts and 2 short voicemails; most reps stop after 1 | S |
| 5 | Build 20 leads at a time when a list is empty; move churches to a slow lane | Previews expire in 30 days; 25 church leads with 4 required to-dos each sat untouched | S |
| 6 | Walk in first for restaurants with 100+ reviews, 2:30 to 4:30 PM; the log card asks who you met and when they are in | The only real interest came from visits; the "ask for" name is filled on 1 lead in 127 | S |
| 7 | Book by phone, close in person or by a link the owner asked for; file the Alabama telemarketing license | The license is $500 a year plus $50 a caller; a phone close with a texted checkout link sits outside the exemption | S plus a lawyer read |
| 8 | Read menus, price boards and hours signs from a photo taken at the demo | Every restaurant needs its menu typed before going live, salons their prices; this makes "live today" possible | M |
| 9 | Attach a second product at sign-up (Google profile setup or review cards) and ask again at month 3; show 3 extras at the counter, not 17 | A second product halves churn; clients not upsold by month 3 churned 62% | S |
| 10 | A dated first-30-days plan on every new client, a 90-day results call, and Stripe's card updater and retries | The first month decides retention; a third of churn is failed cards | M |
| 11 | Read the agreement's five "In short" lines out loud and initial the cancellation line; ask for the yearly upgrade at month 3 | Almost nobody reads terms; the competitors' one-star reviews are all surprise terms; annual prepay retains 30% better when asked later | S |
| 12 | Join the chamber and co-host a "Get Online Day" with the Hanceville SBDC; trade leads with Toast, Clover and Square reps | Warm leads shown at twice the rate of cold ones; the live build is a stage act nobody else has | M |

## What not to change

The core of the business matches what the evidence and the first week both say.

- Preview first, shown in person, signed and paid on the spot. Visits produced the only real interest; the research says the same.
- The plan ladder. $49, $89 and $149 a month with six- or twelve-month terms is a third to a quarter of Thryv and Townsquare and simpler than any of them. Do not add plans.
- The agreement with its "In short" block, Tap to Pay, the publish gate and the sold-design rule. These are the parts a competitor would envy.
- The design system. Owners who skip a website say "not relevant to my industry" far more than anything about looks. Design is no longer why anyone says no, so the generator can rest until a prospect says otherwise.

## Questions for the team

These are the things the numbers cannot tell us and the people making the calls can. Text your answers to Post, or type them into the shared editable copy.

1. When you call a restaurant, who picks up, and how often is the owner actually there? What time of day has worked?
2. Which objection do you hear most: "not relevant to my business", "my nephew does it", price, or "let me think about it"?
3. When you texted a preview link, did it go to the owner's own phone or the number on the listing? Did you watch them open it?
4. What stops you from logging a call the moment it ends? Too many taps, no signal, the card is hard to find, something else?
5. Which parts of the app do you never open? What did you look for and not find?
6. Would an assigned list of 15 to 20 leads a week help, or would it feel like a quota?
7. Which three extras could you pitch at the counter without looking at notes?
8. Churches: does anyone want to work them now, or do we park them until we have paying clients?
9. What would make the first call easier: a one-card script on Home, a practice run, or riding along on a visit?
10. Anything in the changes table you would rank higher or lower, and why?
